Acoustic Event Detection for Illegal Logging Prevention

using Spectral Analysis and LoRa Technology

Team: Paco (thesis lead), Cabatino, Dador

Problem

Remote forests often lack cellular coverage, and satellite monitoring detects forest loss only after it happens.

Solution

A sensor node listens for 2-stroke chainsaws, classifies the sound on the device, and sends an alert over LoRa without cellular or internet service.

How it works

  1. 01  A GY-MAX4466 microphone captures ambient sound.
  2. 02  A Raspberry Pi Pico (RP2040) samples the audio using DMA.
  3. 03  FFT-based Mel-filterbank energy (MFE) features are extracted.
  4. 04  A quantized int8 CNN, built in Edge Impulse, classifies three classes: chainsaw, noise, and unknown.
  5. 05  An SX1276 LoRa module sends the alert peer-to-peer at 923 MHz.
  6. 06  A Pico and LoRa receiver connects to a laptop over USB.

Hardware

Transmitter (left) and receiver (right) schematics, EasyEDA. Tap to enlarge.

  • Power: each node runs from an 18650 Li-ion cell charged through a TP4056 USB-C module. The TP4056 output feeds the Pico's VSYS input through a 1N4007 diode, and the Pico's 3.3 V output powers the microphone and LoRa modules.
  • Audio: the MAX4466 output feeds the RP2040's ADC (GP26 / ADC0).
  • Radio: the SX1276 connects over SPI (GP16 to GP19), with DIO0 and RST on GP20 and GP21.

Design targets

Not yet measured results:

  • Detection radius of 80–150 m per node
  • LoRa link of up to 1 km

Engineering challenge

The transmitter gave false alerts in rain. The planned fix is to record rain audio through the transmitter itself and add it to the classifier's training data.

Next steps

  • PCB design
  • Better antennas
  • Enclosures
  • Field testing
  • Receiver dashboard: status, alert log, RSSI, location, alarm
